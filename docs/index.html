Royal

Reddit, RSS feeds, and your YouTube subscriptions in one feed. Free, open source, and no tracking.

Source App Store Saved

Free for iPhone · Requires iOS 17 or later

One feed, the way you like it

RSS

Any RSS or Atom feed or bring your list over from another reader with OPML

Reddit

Best or Top (today) from the subreddits you follow, or just one of them picked from the menu. Capped at five posts per subreddit, with pinned posts left out and NSFW posts hidden unless you turn them on.

YouTube

Your subscriptions or your Recommended videos, played right in the app, in Safari, or in the YouTube app. Keep browsing your feeds in a compact list while a video plays.

The All feed

Everything together, starting with what's new since your last refresh. Pull up at the end to mark it all read, and pull up again to look back through earlier posts.

Save for later

Swipe left on any post, or press and hold it, to save it. Saved posts sync through your own iCloud account to your other devices and the web.

Make it yours

Large cards or compact rows for RSS, Reddit and YouTube, seven text sizes, and your choice of what a tap opens: the link or the comments on Reddit, the description or the link on RSS. Turn off any source you don’t use.

No setup, no server

RSS needs no account at all, and Reddit and YouTube sign in on their own pages inside the app. No API keys, no accounts with us. Royal talks to each site directly.

Beyond your iPhone

Saved on the web

See, open, and unsave the posts you've saved from any browser. Sign in with the Apple Account your iPhone uses for iCloud; your posts come straight from your private iCloud, with no Royal server in between.

Privacy Policy

Effective November 12, 2026

In short: Royal does not collect, store, or share any of your data. There is no Royal server, no analytics, no advertising, and no tracking. The developer never sees your accounts, your feeds, or what you read.

This policy covers the Royal app for iPhone and iPad (“Royal”, “the app”) and the Royal Saved website at royal.tinyk.ing/saved/. Royal is open source, so everything described here can be checked in the source code.

Information we collect

None. Royal has no backend. It contains no analytics, crash-reporting, or advertising SDKs, and it has no third-party dependencies at all. The developer does not receive any personal information, usage data, identifiers, or diagnostics from the app.

Signing in to Reddit and YouTube

When you sign in, you do so on Reddit’s or Google’s own sign-in page, shown inside the app. Your username and password go straight to that service; Royal does not read or store them. The service sets login cookies, which are kept on your device in the app’s private web storage, just as Safari would keep them. Royal uses those cookies only to load your own feeds from that service.

Choosing Log Out in Settings deletes that service’s cookies and website data from the app. Deleting the app removes all of it.

Services the app connects to

To show your feeds, Royal connects directly from your device to:

  • Reddit (reddit.com) for your subscribed subreddits and their posts
  • The RSS feeds you add, on their own sites (by default hnrss.org, krebsonsecurity.com, pitchfork.com and theverge.com), with no cookies or logins
  • YouTube / Google (youtube.com, ytimg.com) for your subscriptions, recommendations, thumbnails, and video playback
  • The websites that host post images and the links you choose to open

Like any app or browser, these requests reveal your IP address and device information to those services, and they handle it under their own privacy policies: Reddit, Google (YouTube), and the sites of the feeds you add. Royal does not send these services anything beyond what is needed to load your feeds and videos.

Data stored on your device

  • Settings such as layout, text size, and how links open.
  • Your RSS feed list, and the latest posts from each feed, so the app opens with them.
  • Recently seen post IDs and the time of your last refresh, so the All feed can show what’s new.
  • Login cookies for Reddit and YouTube, as described above.

This data stays on your device and is removed when you delete the app.

Saved posts and iCloud

Posts you save, and which posts the All feed has shown, are stored on your device and in your personal iCloud account (its private CloudKit database) so they appear on your other devices and, for saved posts, on Royal Saved. This data is held by Apple under Apple’s Privacy Policy and is not accessible to the developer. You can turn off iCloud for Royal in the Settings app.

The Royal Saved website

Royal Saved shows your saved posts in a web browser. You sign in on Apple’s own page; the site then talks only to Apple’s CloudKit service, reading and writing your saved posts in the same private iCloud database the app uses. It keeps your CloudKit sign-in and a copy of your saved posts in your browser’s local storage, and choosing Sign out removes both from that browser. The site has no server of its own and no analytics, and sets no cookies of its own. Videos you play on it are embedded from YouTube, which handles them under Google’s privacy policy.

Tracking

Royal does not track you across apps or websites, and does not share data with data brokers or advertisers.

Children

Royal is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from anyone, including children.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version will be posted on this page with a new effective date. Every past version is available in the project’s change history.
